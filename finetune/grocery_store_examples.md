Little India: Small Indian grocery store in dense urban area. Lots of snacks and dried foods, small produce selection for specialty items. Stocks lentils and rices of all sorts. 

United Noodle: Small east-asian grocery store for specialty items. Good Chinese, Japanese and Korean selection, mostly of jarred, dried and frozen foods, but with an aisle of fresh asian vegetables too.

Minneapolis Farmers Market: seasonal vegetables farmed locally. No jarred or dried ingredients. 

Whole Foods: Large upscale national supermarket chain. Broad fresh produce and butcher counter, strong organic and natural selection, bulk bins, and a wide international aisle. Carries most specialty ingredients a mainstream chain would skip, but at a premium.

Trader Joe's: Mid-sized national chain with a deliberately small, mostly private-label selection. Good for staples, frozen prepared foods, nuts and cheese. Poor for specific branded or specialty ingredients — if they don't make their own version of it, they don't carry it.

Target: General merchandise big-box store with a grocery section. Mainstream national brands, standard produce, dairy and pantry staples. No meaningful international or specialty selection.

Seward Coop. High priced food co-op. Good for bulk items (nuts, seeds, baking ingredients) and specialty vegan products. 

Costco: Warehouse club selling in bulk only. Large cuts of meat, big bags of rice and flour, cheese wheels, olive oil by the gallon. Nothing in a small package, no specialty ingredients, limited produce variety but cheap per pound.

Aldi: Small discount chain with a tight private-label assortment. Basic produce, dairy, canned goods and frozen vegetables at low prices. No butcher counter, no bulk bins, nothing specialty.

Cub Foods: Large mainstream regional supermarket. Full produce, meat and deli departments, national brands throughout, a short Mexican and Asian aisle. Dependable for anything ordinary, thin for anything not.

Kowalski's Market: Upscale local supermarket. Good cheese counter, house-made pasta and sausage, strong bakery, decent European imports. Produce is fresh but the selection is conventional.

Holy Land: Middle Eastern grocer and deli. Olives, pickles, tahini, bulgur, za'atar and sumac, halal butcher counter, fresh pita baked on site. Small produce section leaning toward herbs, eggplant and cucumbers.

Mercado Central: Mexican and Central American market. Dried and fresh chiles, masa, queso fresco and crema, cactus paddles, tomatillos. Butcher sells thin-cut beef and pork for grilling. No European or Asian goods.

Shuang Hur: East Asian supermarket, larger and cheaper than the specialty shops. Live tanks for fish and crab, whole ducks, an entire aisle of soy and fish sauces, fresh noodles, Chinese greens by the armful.

Tienda La Raza: Corner bodega serving a Latino neighborhood. Limited but exact: dried beans, canned chipotles, Maseca, Mexican Coke, a small cooler of cheeses. Produce is a few crates near the register.

Coastal Seafoods: Fishmonger. Whole and filleted fish, shellfish, smoked fish, fish stock and bones for broth. Nothing else beyond lemons, capers and a few sauces.

Clancey's Meats: Neighborhood butcher shop. Whole-animal butchery, house charcuterie, bones and fat for rendering, stock. Will cut to order. Carries almost no produce or pantry goods.

Wedge Community Co-op: Member-owned co-op. Organic produce, bulk grains and spices, local dairy and eggs, a good cheese case. Strong on whole foods, weak on anything processed or imported.

Costco Business Center: Restaurant supply warehouse. Case quantities of oil, flour, canned tomatoes, commercial-grade sheet pans and containers. Produce sold by the case only. No retail-sized anything.

Penzeys Spices: Spice shop only. Whole and ground spices, blends, vanilla and extracts, all fresher and stronger than supermarket jars. No food otherwise.

Surdyk's: Wine shop with an attached cheese and specialty foods counter. Imported cheeses, cured meats, anchovies, vinegars, good olive oil and chocolate. No produce, no meat beyond charcuterie.

Hmong Village Market: Indoor market of small vendors. Southeast Asian herbs sold in bunches — lemongrass, Thai basil, pandan — plus bitter melon, long beans, live poultry, and prepared foods. Cash is easier than card.

Sun Foods: Discount supermarket serving a mixed immigrant neighborhood. Unusually broad for the price: Mexican, Somali, Hmong and Vietnamese goods side by side, halal meat counter, cheap produce in volume.

Kim's Oriental Market: Korean grocery, one room. Kimchi by the tub, gochujang and doenjang, short ribs pre-cut for galbi, perilla leaves and Korean radish. Little outside Korean cooking.

Fresh Thyme: Mid-sized natural foods chain. Bulk bins, organic produce, a modest meat counter, plenty of supplements. Prices below the upscale chains, selection narrower.

Broders' Cucina: Italian deli and market. Imported pasta, tinned tomatoes, parmigiano, prosciutto, good bread. Deep in one cuisine and empty outside it.

Ranch 99: Large pan-Asian chain supermarket. Everything the small shops carry plus scale — bulk rice, full aisles of Japanese, Korean, Thai and Filipino goods, live seafood, an in-store bakery.
